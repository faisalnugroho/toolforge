Frame-level AI-video detection you can pipeline

The NVIDIA Synthetic Video Detector (SVD) is a NIM microservice, first announced at SIGGRAPH 2026 and expanded at IBC 2026, that assesses the probability that footage is authentic versus AI-generated. NVIDIA reports accuracy of 99.3% for text-to-video content and 97.7% for image-to-video, with the largest gains on difficult image-to-video cases. What makes it more than a research demo is distribution: Dalet embeds it in a secure cloud verification workflow for newsrooms, TwelveLabs built it into its Compliance application for frame-level authenticity signals, and Wowza distributes it through the Wowza Video Intelligence Framework for real-time analysis of live feeds — on-prem, edge, cloud, or air-gapped.

Who it's for: Newsroom verification desks, content-authentication teams, digital-forensics units, and streaming platforms that need to screen incoming video at scale.

Key features

eye Probability scoring, not binary verdicts

Returns the probability footage is AI-generated with explainable indicators, so editorial teams keep a human review step instead of trusting a yes/no flag.

zap Real-time and batch modes

Analyze live video feeds in real time via Wowza or submit files through Dalet's secure cloud workflow — the same detector covers broadcast and post-production review.

layered Frame-level integration

TwelveLabs Compliance layers SVD signals under its own compliance screening, so authenticity checks ride along with regional and custom compliance standards in one pass.

server Deploy anywhere NIM runs

On-premises, edge, cloud, hybrid, or fully air-gapped deployment — a hard requirement for news organizations that cannot ship source footage externally.

shield SIGGRAPH-to-IBC maturation

Introduced at SIGGRAPH 2026 and hardened through IBC 2026 with accuracy gains concentrated on the hardest image-to-video cases, the ones that fool casual review.

link Named production integrations

Dalet (newsroom verification), Wowza (35,000+ streaming deployments), TwelveLabs (compliance screening) — the microservice ships inside products people already run.

The honest take

✓ What works

  • 99.3% / 97.7% detection accuracy reported by NVIDIA on T2V / I2V content
  • Distributed through real broadcast products (Dalet, Wowza, TwelveLabs) rather than API-only
  • Probability scores with explainable indicators preserve human editorial review
  • Air-gapped deployment option for sensitive newsroom footage

✗ What doesn't

  • Accuracy figures are NVIDIA self-reported, not independently audited
  • Adversarial drift: generators improve, detection is a moving target
  • No published per-video pricing — enterprise NVIDIA procurement
  • Detection is probabilistic — high-stakes verdicts still need corroborating signals

Verdict

SVD is the most production-ready synthetic-video detector for broadcast and streaming pipelines, mainly because it ships inside Dalet, Wowza, and TwelveLabs rather than as an isolated API. Newsrooms and platforms that must screen user and agency footage at scale should pilot it now; smaller teams without NVIDIA infrastructure will get more value from API-first detectors like Reality Defender or Hive first.
