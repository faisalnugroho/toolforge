GLM-5.3: same base model as 5.2, every gain from post-training

GLM-5.3 is Z.ai's flagship language model released August 14, 2026 - a 743-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts (the same base model as GLM-5.2) where every headline gain comes from post-training rather than a new architecture. It is built for coding, agentic workflows, terminal-based tasks and long-horizon reasoning, and it posts open-source state of the art on Terminal Bench 3.0 and Agents' Last Exam. The startling part is the jump: Terminal Bench 3.0 goes 4.6 to 28.3, DeepSWE 46.2 to 66.9, and Agents' Last Exam CLI 23.8 to 28.5. On GDPval-AA v2 - the one row Z.ai didn't run itself, scored by Artificial Analysis - it posts 1769, ahead of Claude Fable 5 at 1743 and GPT-5.6 Sol at 1730. MIT-licensed open weights followed around August 28.

Who it's for: Developers who want a frontier-class coding and agentic model they can self-host under MIT, teams building long-horizon terminal agents, and anyone who needs top-tier vulnerability analysis without closed-vendor gating.

Key features

💻 Post-training coding leap

Terminal Bench 3.0 jumps 4.6 to 28.3 and DeepSWE 1.1 goes 46.2 to 66.9 - a 50% improvement on Z.ai's in-house Code Bench over GLM-5.2 from post-training alone, no new base model.

🔒 Emergent cyber capabilities

84.5% on CyberGym (up from 77.2% for 5.2) - best on the benchmark, ahead of Claude Mythos 5 (83.8%) and GPT-5.6 Sol (83.6%) - plus deep ExploitBench reasoning on real vulnerabilities.

🧠 1M-token context, always-on reasoning

A 1.3M-token context window with reasoning that cannot be disabled, controllable effort tiers (low/high/max, max default) - built for sustained long-horizon work.

📜 MIT open weights

The 743B weights shipped under MIT around August 28, 2026 - the most permissive license of any current frontier-class model, with 26 OpenRouter providers competing the price down to $0.70/$2.20.

🏆 Independent-verified intelligence

GDPval-AA v2 of 1769 - scored by Artificial Analysis, not Z.ai - ahead of Fable 5 (1743) and GPT-5.6 Sol (1730), the strongest independent datapoint from launch day.

🤖 Agentic + terminal-native

Open-source SOTA on Agents' Last Exam CLI (23.8 to 28.5) and tuned for terminal-based tasks - the exact profile of Claude Code / Codex-class tooling.

The honest take

✓ What works

  • Post-training leap is real: Terminal Bench 3.0 4.6 to 28.3 and DeepSWE 46.2 to 66.9 on the same base model
  • MIT open weights at frontier scale - self-host without license lawyers
  • 84.5% CyberGym, best on the benchmark, ahead of both closed-lab rivals
  • Independent GDPval-AA v2 1769 beats Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol
  • 26 OpenRouter providers: $0.70/$2.20 with automatic failover
  • 1M-token context with flat pricing across the window and automatic context caching

✗ What doesn't

  • Reasoning always on and cannot be disabled - you pay reasoning tokens on every call
  • GLM-5.3-Flash serves at ~49 tokens/sec vs ~86 for GLM-5.3 - throughput varies wildly by variant and provider
  • No published per-token price at launch - the $0.70/$2.20 figure came from third-party OpenRouter listing
  • Z.ai is a Beijing-based, Hong Kong-listed vendor - some procurement teams will need a policy exception
  • A 743B MoE needs serious hardware to self-host; the MIT license doesn't make it cheap to run

Verdict

GLM-5.3 is the strongest open-weights coding model available right now, and the fact that all its gains come from post-training on an unchanged base makes it a genuinely interesting research datapoint too. If you want frontier coding, terminal agents and even security analysis without a closed vendor, this is the model to benchmark first - and the MIT license plus 26-provider competition make the economics hard to argue with. Watch the throughput on whichever variant you pick.
