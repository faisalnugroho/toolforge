Co-editing with Gemini, wired into Workspace

Gemini Canvas brings the side-by-side editing pattern to Google's AI suite. Open a canvas inside the Gemini app, draft or paste in a doc, and let Gemini rewrite, expand, or restructure sections inline. The killer feature is export: one click pushes the canvas into Google Docs, where it inherits your Workspace sharing, comments, and version control. For teams already paying for Google One AI Premium, this is the AI-editor default.

Who it's for: Google Workspace shops, students living in Docs, and anyone whose deliverable already has to land in Google Drive.

Key features

W Workspace export

One click converts the canvas to a native Google Doc with full sharing and comments.

E Inline rewrite

Highlight and ask Gemini to adjust tone, trim, or expand without touching the rest.

C Code + prose

The same surface handles a Python script or a marketing brief.

R Research panel

Pulls in cited sources from Google Search alongside the draft.

A Audio read-back

Have the draft read aloud to catch rhythm and tone issues.

T Templates

Start from a resume, blog post, or product spec template instead of a blank page.

The honest take

✓ What works

  • Native export to Google Docs is unbeatable for Workspace teams
  • Free tier in the consumer Gemini app is genuinely usable
  • Search-grounded drafts cite real sources
  • Available on Android, iOS, and web on day one

✗ What doesn't

  • Reasoning slightly behind GPT-5 on long-form code tasks
  • Canvas panel can feel cramped on smaller screens
  • Fewer structured-edit shortcuts than ChatGPT Canvas
  • Occasional over-eager rewrites that need rollback

Verdict

If your team lives in Google Workspace, this is the default pick -- the Docs export alone is worth the switch. If you are ecosystem-agnostic and want the strongest co-editing UX, ChatGPT Canvas still leads by a nose.
