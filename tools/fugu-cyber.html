A cyber specialist that beats frontier models at their own game

Sakana AI launched Fugu-Cyber on July 21, 2026 as a cybersecurity-focused update to its Fugu orchestration model. Like the rest of the Fugu family it is an orchestration endpoint, not a new frontier model: it reads a query, builds an agentic scaffold on the fly, and delegates sub-tasks to specialist models in a pool, coordinated through the TRINITY Thinker/Worker/Verifier framework and the reinforcement-trained Conductor. Sakana reports 86.9% on CyberGym and 72.1% on CTI-REALM — self-reported and un-replicated, but edging past GPT-5.5-Cyber’s 85.6% and Claude Mythos Preview’s 83.1% on CyberGym. Access is deliberately gated: manual approval, a defensive-use acceptable-use policy, Token Plan only, no EU/EEA availability, and no open weights.

Who it's for: Security operations centers, red-team-adjacent defenders, and compliance-bound enterprises that can pass Sakana’s manual approval process.

The honest take

✓ What works

  • Best reported CyberGym score at launch

  • Sakana positions it as API plus human expertise, not an autonomy black box

✗ What doesn't

  • All benchmark numbers are self-reported and un-replicated
  • Manual approval gate slows procurement

Verdict

For approved security teams, Fugu-Cyber is the current reported leader on CyberGym and CTI-REALM, and the orchestration approach — delegating to specialists under a defensive AUP — fits how SOCs actually work. Everyone else should benchmark the public alternatives first: GPT-5.5-Cyber and Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber (Fairwind) cover similar ground with different access models.
