Multimodal flash-class weights you can actually afford

Ling 3.0 Flash VL (InclusionAI, September 2026) strengthens the Ling 3.0 Flash base with native visual perception — image and video input — plus advanced visual-agent capabilities. The natively multimodal MoE runs 124B total parameters with about 5.5B active per token, keeps a 262K context window, and ships as open MIT-licensed weights on Hugging Face in BF16, FP8, FP4, and INT4 builds with SGLang and vLLM serving recipes. A hybrid instant/reasoning mode toggles explicit reasoning for harder problems, and it is free to call on OpenRouter.

Who it's for: Builders who want a free, self-hostable multimodal model for vision-heavy agents, document flows, and video understanding.

Key features

👁️ Native vision

Image and video input plus visual-agent capabilities — not a bolted-on caption loop.

🧩 124B/5.5B MoE

Big-model capacity with roughly 5.5B parameters active per token keeps serving costs flash-class cheap.

🔓 MIT open weights

BF16, FP8, FP4, and INT4 builds with SGLang and vLLM recipes for local or cloud GPUs.

🎚️ Hybrid reasoning

Toggle explicit thinking mode for hard problems; instant mode for cheap throughput.

The honest take

✓ What works

  • Genuinely free on OpenRouter
  • MIT license — commercial use and self-hosting allowed
  • Video and image input at flash-class cost
  • Quantized builds down to INT4 fit modest GPUs

✗ What doesn't

  • 5.5B active parameters limit the ceiling vs frontier models
  • Third-party rankings are still sparse at launch
  • 262K context trails 1M-token rivals like GLM-5.3-Flash
  • No official SLA — self-host or free tier only

Verdict

The most capable free multimodal model in the flash class right now. For vision-heavy agents and self-hosted pipelines it is an easy default; teams wanting a 1M-token context or frontier reasoning should still budget for a paid API.
