The verdict

This is the open-sovereignty play against the closed frontier, and the gap is narrower than the price tags suggest. GPT-6 Astra is the strongest model money can buy in late 2026: 88.0% on SRE-Bench reverse-engineering (versus GPT-5.6 Sol's 55.9%), 39.0% on the contamination-controlled ExploitBench (Sol scored 5.5%), and the top of the computer-use arena. If your product needs the absolute best agentic reasoning and you can absorb $10/$50 per 1M, there is no substitute — that's literally its market.

Nemotron 3 Ultra argues a different case: 550B total with only 55B active per token, a hybrid LatentMoE Mamba-2 architecture tuned for NVIDIA silicon, a 1M-token context window that beats Astra's, and a price of $0.50/$2.20 hosted — roughly 1/20th of Astra's input rate. The OpenMDW-1.1 license with published training-data composition is the transparency play: enterprise RAG, regulated industries, and anyone building on DGX or RTX hardware can self-host and never send a token off-prem. It's not the smartest model on the board, but it may be the smartest per dollar for long-context work.

Our pick: Astra for the hard 10% of calls where frontier quality changes the outcome; Nemotron 3 Ultra for the routine 90% — especially where 1M context, open weights, or data sovereignty matter more than the last few points of benchmark score.

Winner: GPT-6 Astra at the frontier; Nemotron 3 Ultra on price, context, and control
