The verdict

These two are the extreme ends of Alibaba’s open-weight world — and the comparison is really about what a dollar buys. Ling 3.0 Flash is a fraction machine: a 124B MoE with 5.1B active parameters that matches or beats Ant’s own trillion-parameter flagship on most benchmarks, toggles reasoning on demand, and costs $0.021/$0.063 per 1M on OpenRouter — you can run a week of agent sessions for the price of a coffee. Qwen3-Max is a scale machine: 2.4T parameters (95B active), post-trained on Coding & Cowork for 10+ days of autonomous coding, a native 1M-token context, and open weights — but served at $2/$6 per 1M.

The honest split: for token-heavy coding agents, batch refactors, and startup-budget self-hosting, Ling wins on price by two orders of magnitude on the input side — 56.6% SWE-Bench Pro is real quality, not toy-tier, and the MIT license means you can serve it however you like. For long-horizon autonomous coding where the model needs to hold an entire monorepo in context and run for days without derailing, Qwen3-Max’s 1M window and cowork post-training are in a different class — and $2/$6 with $0.17 cache reads is cheap for a 2.4T flagship.

Our pick: Ling 3.0 Flash for cheap coding volume; Qwen3-Max when the job needs frontier autonomy and 1M-context scale.

Winner: Ling 3.0 Flash for price-to-quality; Qwen3-Max for 1M-context autonomous-coding frontier work
