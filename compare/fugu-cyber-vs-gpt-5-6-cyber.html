The verdict

Both are deliberately gated cyber models, and they optimize different halves of the defender workflow. Fugu-Cyber is the benchmark-and-intelligence pick: top reported CyberGym (86.9%, past GPT-5.5-Cyber’s 85.6% and Claude Mythos Preview’s 83.1%) and the CTI-REALM lead at 72.1% for threat-intel extraction, delivered as an orchestration endpoint that builds a Thinker/Worker/Verifier scaffold per query. GPT-5.6-Cyber is the exploit-development pick: 95% completion on OpenAI’s advanced cyber evaluation, ahead of Sol and GPT-5.5-Cyber, with documented real-world V8 zero-days — one became CVE-2026-15903 through coordinated disclosure. The scores are not directly comparable (self-reported vs OpenAI’s internal eval), and neither is buyable off a pricing page: Fugu-Cyber requires Token Plan plus manual approval and excludes the EU/EEA; GPT-5.6-Cyber runs only through Daybreak Red vetting. If your team’s work is CTI analysis and defensive depth, Fugu-Cyber’s reported scores argue for it; if it is turning known vulnerabilities into working exploits under governance, GPT-5.6-Cyber’s CVE track record does.

Winner: Fugu-Cyber for CTI and benchmarked defense; GPT-5.6-Cyber for exploit development with a real CVE track record
