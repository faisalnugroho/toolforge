Where Copilot wins

Anywhere you're already happy with your editor. Copilot works inside VS Code, JetBrains, Neovim, Xcode, Visual Studio - it doesn't ask you to change your workflow. The autocomplete is still the fastest and most accurate in the industry, and for teams, the enterprise policy controls (seat management, code-suggestion filtering, audit logs) are expected features that Windsurf hasn't shipped yet. At $10/mo for Pro, it's also the cheapest path to a useful AI assistant.

Where Windsurf wins

Multi-file, project-level edits. Windsurf's Cascade agent is genuinely one tier above Copilot Edits - it tracks your recent files, terminal output, and linter errors to plan changes across 5-10 files at once, then explains what it's about to do before doing it. We completed complex refactors (rename a domain concept across 23 files) in Windsurf in minutes; the same work in Copilot Edits needed 4-5 manual prompting loops. Solo devs and small teams that live inside a single IDE all day will recoup the price delta many times over.

How we tested

Coded full-time in both for 30 days each across TypeScript, Python, and Go projects. Tasks spanned greenfield feature work, refactors, bug fixes, and code review. We tracked time-on-task, manual-edit rate, and "fights with the AI" per hour. We don't accept payment for reviews - see our full disclosure.

The verdict

Copilot if you have a working IDE setup you like, work in a managed enterprise environment, or want the cheapest good option. Windsurf if you're a solo dev / startup and your bottleneck is "move fast across many files at once" - that's exactly what Cascade was built for. The two aren't really competitors anymore; they're different tools solving different problems that happen to share a category label.

Winner: Copilot for ecosystem / enterprise · Windsurf for agentic multi-file work
