The verdict

Use Runway Gen-6 for professional video production — character consistency across shots, cinematic camera control, motion brush, and director-level tools. Use Sora 4 for photorealistic output, longer clips (up to 20s), and physics-accurate motion at half the price. For filmmakers: Runway. For casual creators: Sora 4.

Winner: Runway Gen-6 for pros, Sora 4 for everyone else

Where Runway Gen-6 wins

Character consistency: Runway Gen-6 maintains character identity across multiple shots and angles — the same person looks the same in every clip. This is a game-changer for narrative video and ad production. Sora 4 struggles with this in multi-shot sequences.

Motion brush and camera control: Runway's motion brush lets you paint specific areas to animate while keeping the rest static. Combined with camera paths (pan, zoom, orbit), you get director-level control that Sora 4's text-only interface can't match.

4K resolution: Runway Gen-6 outputs at 4K, while Sora 4 is capped at 1080p. For professional work, this resolution difference matters.

API access: Runway has a mature API for programmatic video generation. Sora 4's API is still in limited beta.

Where Sora 4 wins

Photorealism: Sora 4 produces the most photorealistic AI video we've seen. Lighting, reflections, skin textures, and physics simulation (cloth, water, hair) are noticeably more realistic than Runway Gen-6.

Longer clips: Sora 4 can generate up to 20-second clips vs Runway's 10 seconds. For storytelling, those extra 10 seconds matter.

Price: Sora 4 is included in ChatGPT Plus ($20/mo), which also gives you GPT-5.1, DALL-E, voice mode, and more. Runway's $35/mo is video-only.

Ease of use: Sora 4's text-to-video interface is simpler — just type a prompt. Runway's advanced controls have a steeper learning curve.
