The verdict

The nano tier used to mean "toy." Both of these models ended that: they are the two cheapest credible agent brains you can rent in 2026, and they split the market on the same line every open-vs-closed comparison draws — sovereignty versus convenience. Nemotron 3 Nano gives you 30B total with 3B active, a 262,144-token window, a literal reasoning on/off switch in the chat template, and NVIDIA's full open stack (weights, datasets, recipes) for anyone who wants to move it onto their own GPU. At $0.05/$0.20 per 1M — with a free OpenRouter endpoint for testing — it is hard to beat on unit economics for RAG, triage, and always-on agent loops.

GPT-5 Nano's pitch is the inverse: nothing to host, nothing to tune, OpenAI-grade trust and tooling from the first call. For teams already on the OpenAI API it slots into existing code with a model-name change, inherits the ecosystem's structured outputs and function calling, and carries the GPT-5 family's quality floor. The trade is what it always is: per-token pricing you don't control, no weights, and your prompts live wherever OpenAI puts them.

Our pick: Nemotron 3 Nano if you can run a benchmark and value optionality — open weights plus a dozen hosts make it the cost and control play. GPT-5 Nano if your roadmap has zero room for infrastructure and the OpenAI stack is already your home turf.

Winner: Nemotron 3 Nano on price and openness; GPT-5 Nano on plug-and-play ecosystem
