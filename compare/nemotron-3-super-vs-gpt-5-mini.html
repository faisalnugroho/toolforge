The verdict

This is the mid-tier collision: the open model engineered for agent swarms versus the closed small model that quietly powers a staggering share of production API traffic. Nemotron 3 Super's architecture is its argument — hybrid Mamba-2 and Transformer layers with a Latent MoE router that calls four experts for the price of one, plus multi-token prediction that NVIDIA reports generates over 50% more tokens than comparable open models. Trained with multi-environment RL across 10+ environments, it posts leading open-model results on AIME 2025, TerminalBench, and SWE-Bench Verified — the agentic benchmarks that predict real agent behavior. At $0.09/$0.45 per 1M, with a free endpoint and a dozen hosts competing on price, it is the default answer for anyone building multi-agent systems on a budget.

GPT-5 mini plays the incumbent card: it is fast, dependable, priced per token without surprises, and wrapped in the OpenAI toolchain most teams already run. Long-horizon coherence and deep tool-use chains are where the gap shows — that is precisely what Super's 1M-scale context window and RL pedigree were built for, and where the mini tier starts paying for retries and scaffolding to compensate.

Our pick: Nemotron 3 Super for anything agentic, multi-step, or context-heavy — especially if self-hosting is on the roadmap. GPT-5 mini for high-volume, well-scoped workloads where OpenAI integration is already the path of least resistance and the extra reasoning headroom would go unused.

Winner: Nemotron 3 Super for agentic depth and cost; GPT-5 mini for ecosystem simplicity
