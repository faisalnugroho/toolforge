The verdict

This is the mid-market knife fight that most API spend actually flows through, and both sides made August 2026 pricing moves that sharpened it. Anthropic launched Sonnet 5 in June at $2/$10 framed as temporary, scheduled a 50% rise to $3/$15 for September 1 — then cancelled it and made the intro price permanent on August 10. One catch: Sonnet 5 runs a new tokenizer that produces roughly 30% more tokens on the same input than Sonnet 4.6, so migration savings aren't linear. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family answers with tiers instead of a single price: Sol holds $5/$30 flagship rates, Terra dropped 20% to $2/$12, and Luna took an 80% cut to $0.20/$1.20 — with explicit cache breakpoints and a 30-minute minimum cache life. Head-to-head at the balanced tier, Sonnet 5's $2/$10 undercuts Terra's $2/$12 with Anthropic's coding pedigree (Red Hat reported perfect root-cause ID on every broken build tested, and Sonnet 5 is the Claude default for Free and Pro). But Luna at $0.20/$1.20 plays in a price class Sonnet can't touch for bulk classification and extraction. The verdict: for agent loops and production coding, Sonnet 5's permanent pricing and default status make it the safest mid-market pick; for cost-matched routing across volumes, GPT-5.6's three tiers give you a lever Anthropic doesn't offer.

Winner: Sonnet 5 for stable mid-market coding; GPT-5.6 for tiered cost routing (Luna is unbeatable on volume)
