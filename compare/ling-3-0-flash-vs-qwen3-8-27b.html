The verdict

These two represent opposite answers to the same question: how do you get frontier-adjacent coding without frontier prices? Ling 3.0 Flash does it with server-side scale — a 124B MoE with only 5.1B parameters active per token (~8% of the network firing on any step), which is how it posts 56.6% on SWE-Bench Pro and still serves at $0.021/$0.063 per 1M on OpenRouter. The hybrid instant/thinking mode toggles reasoning on demand, hierarchical caching cuts time-to-first-token 60–80% on long inputs, and the MIT license covers commercial use. Qwen3.8-27B does it with local density — a 27B vision-language model that fits on a single 24GB consumer GPU at 4-bit quantization, posts 61.7 on SWE-bench Pro (beating Claude Opus 4.6 Max’s 53.4 on Qwen’s card), 84.3 on OSWorld-Verified computer-use, and carries a 262K context extendable to 1M via YaRN — with zero per-token cost and full data locality.

The honest split: if your coding agent runs in the cloud and you pay per token, Ling 3.0 Flash is nearly impossible to beat — a week of agent sessions costs the price of a coffee, and its multilingual (72.4%) and math (93.2 AIME 2026) numbers hold up for general work too. If your constraint is privacy, offline operation, or a hardware budget rather than an API budget, Qwen3.8-27B is the strongest thing that runs on one gaming GPU — and the native multimodal input (text, image, video) makes it a real computer-use agent, not just a chat model. Note the caveats on both sides: Ling’s benchmarks are self-reported with limited independent replication, and Qwen’s card numbers come from Qwen’s own comparison table.

Our pick: Ling 3.0 Flash for cheap hosted coding volume; Qwen3.8-27B for local, offline, and computer-use agents on one GPU.

Winner: Ling 3.0 Flash for hosted cost-efficiency; Qwen3.8-27B for local self-hosted agentic coding
